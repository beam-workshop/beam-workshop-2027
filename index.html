Why this workshop?

Brain science and AI are meeting at a new frontier.

Recent advances in biomedical AI, foundation models, neuroimaging, neural data analysis, and embodied agents are reshaping how we study the brain and how we build intelligent systems. Yet the communities working on brain health, brain-inspired AI, and embodied intelligence often develop their methods and benchmarks separately.

This workshop creates a shared forum for researchers who want to understand the brain, advance biomedical discovery, and design AI systems that perceive, act, adapt, and learn through interaction with bodies and environments.

Scope

Three connected themes

We welcome work that bridges mechanisms, models, data, and embodied behavior.

01

Biomedical brain science

Neuroimaging, neural signals, brain disorders, clinical prediction, intervention modeling, biomarkers, and data-driven neuroscience.

02

AI for the brain

Foundation models for neuroscience and biomedicine, multimodal brain data, interpretable AI, simulation, digital twins, and scientific discovery.

03

Embodied intelligence

Brain-inspired agents, perception-action loops, active learning, world models, robotics, cognitive maps, social interaction, and adaptive behavior.

Call for papers

We invite submissions across brain, body, and intelligence.

Submission details will be announced soon. We welcome empirical, theoretical, clinical, and position papers that connect biomedical brain science with AI and embodied intelligence.

Potential topics include

  • AI for neuroimaging, neural signals, and brain disease modeling
  • Foundation models and multimodal learning for biomedical brain science
  • Interpretable and reliable AI for brain discovery and brain health
  • Brain-inspired representation learning, memory, and reasoning
  • Embodied agents, robotics, active perception, and world models
  • Social cognition, multi-agent interaction, and human-AI systems
  • Benchmarks, datasets, evaluation, ethics, and safety
